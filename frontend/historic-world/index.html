AIZANOI Walk the city at its High Imperial peak — c. AD 225

This V8 reconstruction is built around the published archaeological relationships of Aizanoi: the Temple of Zeus west of the Penkalas, the agora between sanctuary and river, the great bath-palaestra to the northwest, the theatre–stadium farther north, the mosaic bath on the eastern bank, the Macellum toward the southern civic zone, Roman bridges and quays, necropolises, fields and the southern sacred landscape. Excavated facts and inferred urban fabric are deliberately separated.

The city was not reconstructed as a rectangular Roman grid. Monument axes differ from one another, and the Penkalas is treated as the main urban spine.

How to read certainty

High: monument location, principal dimensions or architectural character are directly stated in excavation publications or official archaeological pages.

Medium: placement is well established, but elevation, roof, façade or an incomplete side of the plan is reconstructed.

Inferred: street frontage, houses, market stalls, vegetation, minor shrines, carts, stalls and many everyday details are plausible atmosphere rather than an excavated restitution. Mannequin NPC figures are intentionally omitted in V8.

Important chronological rule

The early 5th-century colonnaded street is not shown as complete in AD 225. The AD 301 layer adds the Macellum Price Edict context; the AD 425 layer introduces the colonnaded street and schematic Late Antique reuse.

Scale

Core monuments are modeled at approximately 1 world unit = 1 metre. The remote Roman dam and Meter Steunene sanctuary are represented as separate compressed landscape zones so a browser walk does not require several real kilometres.

Research base

Sources & reconstruction method

Official Aizanoi excavation pages are used first for monument dimensions and phases; academic publications are used for urban morphology, Penkalas archaeology, funerary material and recent theatre inscriptions. The experience is a historical visualisation, not a substitute for an architectural restitution drawing.

DPU Aizanoi — Temple of Zeus

Construction from AD 92 into the Hadrianic period; 35 × 55 m podium; octastyle pseudodipteros; 42 columns; 9.51 m column height; vaulted lower chamber; land-rent inscriptions.

Open official page
DPU Aizanoi — Theatre–Stadium

Shared stage building, long north–south axis, construction phases from the 1st through 3rd centuries, estimated 13,000 stadium and 20,000 theatre capacity, 14 theatre vomitoria.

Open official page
DPU Aizanoi — Agora & Propylon

Agora between Zeus sanctuary and Penkalas; east–west dimension 97.36 m; west stoa 8.88 m; south stoa 19.20 m; shops with lamp/oil and bone-working evidence; Propylon 30.70 × 14.50 m with 30 steps.

Open official page
DPU Aizanoi — Agora Nymphaeum

Excavated in the northeast corner of the agora; 5.23 m diameter foundation, sequence of marble base/parapet blocks and drainage channel.

Open official page
DPU Aizanoi — Great Bath–Palaestra

Mid-2nd century; minor Imperial bath type; 110 × 145 m palaestra; 18 × 4.70 m natatio, 1 m deep; Hygieia statue; later Byzantine additions.

Open official page
DPU Aizanoi — Mosaic Bath

2nd–3rd century bath with hypocaust, apodyterium, tepidarium, calidarium and frigidarium; satyr and maenad mosaic; later basilical / church reuse.

Open official page
DPU Aizanoi — Colonnaded Street

Early 5th century, estimated original length about 450 m; reused architectural material including elements associated with a 1st-century Temple of Artemis.

Open official page
DPU Aizanoi — Macellum

Circular market building, 13.27 m diameter; food market rather than a literal modern stock exchange; AD 301 Price Edict copied in Greek and Latin on the podium.

Open official page
DPU Aizanoi — Odeon / Bouleuterion

Square-plan building southeast of the Zeus temenos with semicircular seating; interpreted as council hall first and later odeon, with long use into the Byzantine period.

Open official page
DPU Aizanoi — Meter Steunene

Open-air sacred place about 4 km south of the Zeus temple; literary tradition of the Steunos cave; two cylindrical ritual structures identified as taurobolium and criobolium.

Open official page
DPU Aizanoi — Penkalas & Bridges

Four bridges associated with the Eurykles family in the Hadrianic period, fortified riverbanks/quay walls, and a Roman dam about 3 km south of the centre.

Open official page
DPU Aizanoi — History

Roman public-building boom, Panhellenion connection, Late Antique provincial and episcopal history, and later Çavdar Tatar use of the temple as a fort.

Open official page
UNESCO Tentative List — Aizanoi

Overview of Aizanoi's outstanding Roman monuments and its place on Türkiye's World Heritage Tentative List.

Open UNESCO page
Tandoğan & Erdoğan 2020

Key urban-morphology study: the city developed organically on both sides of the Penkalas; monument entrances and axes do not conform to a strict right-angled street grid. Includes settlement and axis plans.

Archaeological and restoration work along the river, bridge improvement project, historic development and the relation of the Penkalas to settlement and agricultural landscape.
