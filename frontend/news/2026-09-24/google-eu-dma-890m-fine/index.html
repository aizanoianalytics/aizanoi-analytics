Google revamps European search results under DMA compliance after €890 million EU antitrust fine

Google has warned users in the European Economic Area that it will modify its site reputation abuse policy for Search, months after the European Commission fined the company €890 million in July for Digital Markets Act breaches on Google Search self-preferencing and Play steering. The ITIF Foundation noted on 3 September that the change shows the recurring cost of DMA compliance for US platforms. The Commission has given Google 60 days to comply or face penalty payments of up to 5 percent of worldwide daily turnover; Google told users its EEA results would be 'lower quality' as a consequence.
