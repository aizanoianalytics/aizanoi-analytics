AI

2026-11-02

Agentic coding is becoming a staffing decision, not a feature

Three releases this week point the same way. GitHub measures agents starting more than 40% of pull requests, DeepSeek's Harness team published a router-worker prototype built to consume as little human attention as possible, and Google is retiring Jules to concentrate on Antigravity. The common thread is organisational rather than technical: the constraint has moved from whether a model can write a patch to who authorises it, who reviews it and how much supervision a team can afford. Each of those questions now has a vendor-shaped answer.

AI

2026-11-02

The training-data argument has moved from principle to syntax

Two disclosures this week show the AI industry negotiating consent by syntax. Google, OpenAI, Microsoft and Anthropic backed one robots.txt policy token so a publisher can refuse training once for every participating provider, though implementation dates run from months away to February 2027. Anthropic separately described self-assessment, in which Claude grades its own conversations. Both are narrow: the token does not stop crawling, and the monitor does not block misuse. They are still the shape of the coming settlement — machine-readable, auditable, and run by the party being audited.

Football

2026-11-02

Small margins, heavy calendars: the season starts to bite

Arsenal's 1-0 win at Anfield was decided by one cut-back and defended by two centre-backs, and it left Liverpool nine points adrift in fifth. In Germany, Bayern and Dortmund remain level after a 2-2 Klassiker in which both lost players to injury, while promoted Elversberg sit among the weekend's biggest winners. The Champions League resumes on Tuesday with Atlético-Bayern and United-Roma. With domestic races tight and European fixtures stacking up, squad depth rather than peak performance is becoming the variable that separates the contenders.

Economy / Markets

2026-11-02

The AI trade now has to survive an election, an oil price and Beijing

Markets enter the week with three variables that no model release controls. Friday's 3.5% fall in the Nasdaq 100 was the sharpest since June and the semiconductor index lost 7.2%, with futures only partly recovering in Asia. Tuesday's US midterms add policy risk, and Brent's slide to $110.19 rests on a diplomatic pause rather than a settlement with Iran. In China, regulators convened banks to widen buyback funding after the Shanghai Composite's 6.3% October decline. The correction, in other words, is being priced against politics and energy as much as against earnings.

Technology

2026-11-02

Memory and compute prices, not model scores, are setting the agenda

The week's technology stories were mostly about cost. Kioxia fell as much as 17% on a cautious outlook, a reminder that memory has become the swing input for servers, PCs and handsets. Microsoft offered buyouts to about 27% of its workforce while funding an AI build-out, and Apple retook the title of most valuable listed company largely by spending less than its peers. Capex discipline is now a product feature: the companies that are cheapest to believe in are being rewarded for restraint, at least until the next earnings cycle tests it.
