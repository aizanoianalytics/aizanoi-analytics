Weekly AI: the Anthropic music-publisher suit reopens the training-data question

Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell's late-August federal complaint against Anthropic lands weeks after the $1.5 billion Bartz judgment and reopens the same question with broader scope: was the source acquisition legal even if training on copyrighted material was not? The publishers frame torrenting and 'flagrant piracy' as the next legal theory, and Anthropic says it will defend itself robustly. Discovery will expose training-data sourcing practices the industry has kept opaque.

Economy / Markets

Weekly Markets: Warsh's hawkish turn reorders the September Fed decision and the AI capex trade

Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech shifted the Fed debate from when to cut to whether to hike, lifting the implied probability of a September move to 55.7% from 35.4% and pushing short-dated Treasury yields and the dollar higher. Equity reaction split along AI exposure: Nvidia gave back Thursday's post-earnings gains as financing costs rose, while Amazon added roughly $110 billion of market value despite a $7.6 billion free-cash-flow outflow. The September dot-plot will determine if the move is tactical or a tighter-for-longer regime.

Technology

Weekly Technology: open-weight frontier models and sovereign AI stacks race past closed APIs

Two releases in the same week reframed the model-supply landscape. Tencent published a 770-billion-parameter open-weights system on August 30, and India's Gnani AI formally launched the Gnani Artha stack built around the 30-billion-parameter Evon 3.3 model and the Plexus agentic platform at Uprashtrapati Bhavan two days earlier. Together they show two parallel pressures on closed-API providers: capability is no longer a moat, and national buyers increasingly want self-hosted systems whose weights, training data and inference paths stay inside their jurisdictions.

Weekly Football: deadline day looms as Premier League clubs chase the last deals of a frenetic window

Premier League clubs have 48 hours to complete signings before Tuesday's 11 p.m. BST deadline, with two-hour deal-sheet grace periods for paperwork filed in time. Weekly AI: agent infrastructure draws capital while foundation-model economics get repriced

The week's deal flow shows where investors are placing the next tranche of AI capital. Rillet raised a $100 million Series C at a $1 billion valuation after a single board update, Instinct reached a $2.5 billion valuation four months after founding, and Socure closed $156 million while absorbing agentic-AI compliance startup Fravity. Infrastructure plays drew comparable attention: Agentrys raised $24.5 million for chip-design agents, Keenable raised $26 million for an agent-optimised web index, and Arga Labs raised $10 million for agent sandboxes.
