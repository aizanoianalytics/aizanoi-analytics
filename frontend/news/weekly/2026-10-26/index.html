AI

AI’s next phase is about control as much as capability

This week’s AI stories point in three directions at once. OpenAI’s reported move away from live internet access underlines the value of controlled training inputs; the Anthropic lawsuit shows that data provenance remains a legal risk; and a fileAI–AWS banking collaboration reflects the pressure to turn models into governed workflows. Taken together, the pattern is less a single breakthrough than an industry learning to trade openness and speed for auditability, permissions and predictable deployment.

Economy / Markets

Markets are pricing politics through oil and currencies

The economic news this week is a study in transmission channels. Brazil’s new political chapter is being framed through fiscal and growth challenges; Asian trading began cautiously as Brent eased from a six-month high; and the Philippine peso touched a record low. None of these developments should be collapsed into one global signal, but together they show how political credibility, commodity prices and imported inflation continue to meet in market pricing.

Technology

Technology stories are moving from demos into physical systems

The week’s technology coverage is notably physical. China’s agricultural technology fair focuses attention on machinery and field adoption; Ukraine and JEF partners are linking defence needs to practical technology cooperation; and the Redwire discussion centres on phased-array systems rather than a consumer launch.
