The Fed's AI problem: an investment boom that raises the prices it promises to lower

Warsh's San Francisco forum on Monday sets out the tension plainly. AI may eventually cut unit costs, but the build-out consumes power, chips, materials and skilled labour now, and those inputs already show up in the inflation data the Fed watches. Governor Lisa Cook says the spending is delaying a return to 2%; governor Michael Barr ties job disruption to adoption speed. With the 10-year near 5.15% and a December increase priced as near certain, the central bank is tightening into the capital cycle it says will be disinflationary.

2026-11-16

Korea is rebuilding its chip supply chain around proximity, not price

SK hynix's 1.4 trillion won supplier programme and the 874.6 billion won Trinity Fab answer a bottleneck: Korean memory leadership still rests on roughly 30% domestic sourcing for materials and equipment, and a result proven on an 8-inch laboratory wafer does not qualify on a 12-inch line. Paying half of development costs up front and compensating suppliers whose projects fail is procurement run as industrial policy. The counterexample is China, where Meituan trained a 1.6-trillion-parameter agent model on Huawei accelerators and published a self-hosting guide with no Nvidia configuration.

England's night in Madrid reframes what the November window is for

Three goals in 57 minutes in Madrid, then half an hour of defending a one-goal lead with ten men after Elliot Anderson's red card: England's 3-2 win over Spain did more for Thomas Tuchel than the autumn friendlies before it.
