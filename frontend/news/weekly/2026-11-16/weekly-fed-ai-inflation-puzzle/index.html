The Fed's AI problem: an investment boom that raises the prices it promises to lower

Warsh's San Francisco forum on Monday sets out the tension plainly. AI may eventually cut unit costs, but the build-out consumes power, chips, materials and skilled labour now, and those inputs already show up in the inflation data the Fed watches. Governor Lisa Cook says the spending is delaying a return to 2%; governor Michael Barr ties job disruption to adoption speed. With the 10-year near 5.15% and a December increase priced as near certain, the central bank is tightening into the capital cycle it says will be disinflationary.
