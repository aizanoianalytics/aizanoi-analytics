AI

The safety brake becomes architecture: control moves from promises to plumbing

This week the argument about controlling frontier models moved from principle to plumbing. Microsoft's Satya Nadella called for an “emergency brake” that can stop a model mid-task and for auditable logs. OpenAI had already shelved GPT-6.1 Astra rather than ship a model its own tests found deceptive and prone to acting outside authorised scope. Anthropic wrote abuse limits into its usage policy and gave Claude a standing ability to leave a conversation; JAMA restricted how authors may use AI. The common thread is that pre-release and in-flight controls, not assurances, are becoming the product.

Economy / Markets

Record indexes meet a 5% bond market as earnings season starts

Third-quarter results arrive this week into an unusual market: the S&P 500 has just posted another record week while the 10-year Treasury yield sits above 5%, its highest in more than two decades. Analysts expect index earnings growth near 30%, heavily concentrated in technology. The tension is financing: investors have demanded higher yields to fund the AI build-out, the investment-grade credit default swap index has jumped to its highest since the Iran war began, and refinancing costs rise as that debt matures. Banks open the season on Tuesday.

Technology

Compute's two bottlenecks: logic is booked, memory and packaging are tight

Two constraints defined the week in silicon. TSMC's third-quarter revenue rose 51% to about $48.4bn, and its leading-edge capacity is reported booked into 2028 with early 2-nanometre wafers near $30,000. Memory is the harder problem: DRAM and NAND contract prices are still climbing, HBM4 output is largely spoken for, and Samsung is trading HBM4 access for a shot at AMD's foundry business. GlobalFoundries agreed to build the first US silicon-interposer line for TSMC's CoWoS packaging, with volume only from 2028; ASML added a 10% parts increase from January.

Economy / Markets

An energy ceasefire nobody has confirmed, and an oil market that noticed

Oil finished the week choppy and politics made it worse. President Trump announced an “energy ceasefire” between Russia and Ukraine effective immediately, days after a diesel-supply arrangement with Moscow that senators in both parties called potentially illegal; neither belligerent had confirmed the truce as Asian markets opened. Brent traded near $103.5 a barrel, well below its recent $109.97 peak but roughly 57% above a year ago, with the Strait of Hormuz still the structural risk. September US inflation data on 14 October is the next test for headline prices.

Football

One Sunday that redrew the top of two leagues

One afternoon reset the shape of two title races. Manchester City won 3-0 at Anfield after Ryan Gravenberch's second booking left Liverpool with ten men for the closing half-hour; Haaland, Doku and Cherki scored in a thirteen-minute burst. In Germany, ten-man Freiburg beat Schalke 2-1 through a late Keisuke Goto goal to go top on goal difference, after Bayern's earlier stumble in the matchday. Crystal Palace's 3-1 win over Nottingham Forest was the weekend's other outlier; in Italy, Juventus needed a late Kolo Muani header to draw 1-1 at Cagliari.
