TSMC stops taking advanced orders from Chinese customers as the new rule bites

People familiar with the matter told Reuters that TSMC has told Chinese customers to stop ordering chips at 16nm and below unless they hold US export licences, widening a cutoff that previously sat at 7nm. The Commerce Department's interim final rule on remote access to advanced semiconductors took effect on 19 November and presumes advanced chips are controlled, shifting the burden of proof onto customers. TSMC has issued no statement. Affected designs are pushed towards 28nm and 40nm work, while nodes at 7nm and below produced 79% of TSMC's third-quarter wafer revenue.
