# Athens · 450–430 BCE — research notes A source-led reconstruction of Classical Athens during the Periclean building programme. The historical frame is 450–430 BCE; the rendered 3D city is deliberately anchored to a late visual snapshot around **432–430 BCE** so buildings begun after 430 are not back-projected into an earlier city. The page intentionally loads no CDN assets, no analytics, and no map tiles; it only links to public research material. ## How to use This page is the static, non-WebGL version of the same source material the 3D experience consumes. If your browser disables WebGL, you still get the methodology below and the distinction between documented, plausible and atmospheric reconstruction. ## Reconstruction methodology - **Named monuments and major roads** are placed from archaeological / documented source records. - **Rendered visual snapshot** is c. 432–430 BCE inside the broader 450–430 BCE historical frame. A structure begun after 430 BCE is not shown as a finished building merely because it belongs to the later fifth century. - **Domestic urban massing** outside the named monuments is deliberately schematic: it gives city scale without claiming to reconstruct individual houses. - **Terrain** preserves the Acropolis rock, Areopagus, Pnyx, Hill of the Nymphs and major valley relationships; local elevation is illustrative, not surveyed. - **Modern overlay** is a schematic alignment layer, not a claimed modern cadastral map. - **Audio** is opt-in and procedural only. ## Core spatial evidence - ASCSA Agora Excavations — [agora.ascsa.gr](https://www.agora.ascsa.gr/) and [agoraexcavations.org](https://www.agoraexcavations.org/) - Travlos, J., *Pictorial Dictionary of Ancient Athens* — standard architectural/topographic reference (library access). - Perseus Digital Library — [perseus.tufts.edu](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/) - ToposText — [topostext.org](https://topostext.org/) - Pleiades — [pleiades.stoa.org](https://pleiades.stoa.org/) ## Period framing The wider product frame covers **450–430 BCE**, the Periclean city between the Thirty Years' Peace and the outbreak of the Peloponnesian War / Plague boundary. The actual visual restitution is **c. 432–430 BCE**: - the Parthenon and Propylaea are complete by this endpoint; - the later Classical Temple of Athena Nike (426–421 BCE) is not yet present; - the Erechtheion (421–406 BCE) is not yet present; - the Athenian Asklepieion founded in 420/19 BCE is not yet present; - the later-fifth-century Pompeion is not yet present; - the older/repaired Athena Polias precinct and earlier Athena Nike cult setting remain represented where appropriate. This is a deliberate chronology safeguard, not an attempt to imply that every element of Athens was frozen on one exact day. ## Major entries in the c. 432–430 visual snapshot - **Akropolis**: Parthenon (447–432 BCE, complete); Propylaea (437–432 BCE, complete); repaired Old Temple of Athena Polias precinct; Athena Nike bastion / earlier shrine rather than the later Ionic temple; Chalkotheke; sanctuary of Artemis Brauronia. - **South slope of the Akropolis**: Theatre of Dionysus represented conservatively with an earthen orchestra and substantial timber *ikria* / bleacher language rather than the later fourth-century monumental stone theatre; Odeion of Pericles (ca. 443 BCE). The later Athenian Asklepieion is omitted. - **Agora of Athens**: Hephaisteion treated as still working toward its later-fifth-century completion; Stoa of Zeus Eleutherios treated conservatively as working/incomplete near the endpoint; Royal Stoa; Stoa Poikile; Bouleuterion; Metroon; Tholos; Panathenaic Way. - **Outside the walls**: Kerameikos cemetery, Sacred Gate and Dipylon Gate; later Pompeion omitted; Pnyx; Areopagus; Olympieion represented as the earlier sanctuary rather than the later Roman giant; Academy landscape. - **Piraeus**: Hippodamian planning context, harbour districts, shipshed areas and the Long Walls connection represented schematically where exact restitution is uncertain. ## High-priority chronology references - Temple of Athena Nike — [Acropolis Museum: Temple of Athena Nike](https://www.theacropolismuseum.gr/en/other-monuments-periklean-building-programme/temple-athena-nike) - Erechtheion / older Athena Polias precinct — [Hellenic Ministry of Culture: Acropolis / Erechtheion](https://odysseus.culture.gr/h/3/eh3530.jsp?obj_id=2384) - Theatre of Dionysus — [Hellenic Ministry of Culture: Theatre of Dionysus Eleuthereus](https://odysseus.culture.gr/h/2/eh251.jsp?obj_id=10341) - Temple of Hephaistos — [Hellenic Ministry of Culture: Temple of Hephaistos](https://odysseus.culture.gr/h/2/eh251.jsp?obj_id=6621) - Kerameikos — [Hellenic Ministry of Culture: Kerameikos](https://odysseus.culture.gr/h/3/eh352.jsp?obj_id=2392) - Asklepieion — [Acropolis Museum / South Slope material](https://www.theacropolismuseum.gr/) ## Additional orientation references - Agora of Athens — [ASCSA Agora Excavations](https://www.agora.ascsa.gr/) - Perseus Digital Library — [perseus.tufts.edu](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/) - ToposText — [topostext.org](https://topostext.org/) - Pleiades — [pleiades.stoa.org](https://pleiades.stoa.org/) ## Evidence policy The model distinguishes: - `archaeological` — physical / excavated evidence; - `documented` — textual or epigraphic evidence supported by context; - `plausible` — scholarly/inferred massing used to make the city inhabitable without claiming exact restitution; - `atmospheric` — light, sound and evocative detail that is not itself evidence.
