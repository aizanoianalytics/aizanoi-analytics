# Classical Athens 450–430 BCE — source-led ledger

## Executive summary

This ledger collects the named monuments, civic spaces, walls, gates, harbours and hydrology that a future agent needs to rebuild a navigable Classical Athens between 450 and 430 BCE. The geographic model is the Attic peninsula with two connected cities: the walled town on and around the Acropolis rock (with the Agora to the north-west, the South Slope beneath the rock, the Kerameikos cemetery outside the Dipylon / Sacred Gates, the Pnyx and Areopagus hills, the Olympieion in the Ilissos valley, and the Academy grove to the north-west), and the harbour city of Piraeus connected by the parallel Long Walls corridor. Every entry below carries an explicit evidence level (`archaeological`, `documented`, or `plausible`) and a working source URL. The Plague of Athens begins at the boundary of this period (430 BCE) and is therefore not depicted.

## ACROPOLIS

- **Parthenon** (Greek: Παρθενών; modern: Parthenon)
  Period: 447–432 BCE · Region: acropolis · State: new
  Evidence: archaeological
  Position / footprint: ~69.5 × 30.9 m; cella 29.8 × 19.2 m; height 13.72 m (Wikipedia, citing academic sources)
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenon
  Description: Octastyle peripteral Doric temple in Pentelic marble, built under Iktinos and Kallikrates with Phidias overseeing sculpture. Built on the euthynteria of the Older Parthenon destroyed in 480 BCE; finished in 432 BCE, just before the outbreak of the Peloponnesian War.

- **Older Parthenon (foundation footprint)**
  Period: pre-480 BCE; foundation line reused · Region: acropolis · State: inferred
  Evidence: documented
  Position / footprint: under the present Parthenon
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenon
  Description: The Older Parthenon, sometimes called the Pre-Parthenon or Hekatompedon, was destroyed by the Achaean Persians in 480 BCE; its euthynteria was reused as the stylobate foundation of the new Parthenon.

- **Propylaea** (Greek: Προπύλαια; modern: Beulé Gate + central building)
  Period: 437–432 BCE · Region: acropolis · State: new
  Evidence: archaeological
  Position / footprint: ~22 m wide (central building)
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propylaea
  Description: Monumental Doric gateway by Mnesicles. Begun once the Parthenon was nearly finished; the eastern Pinakotheke wing was completed but the northern Pinakotheke was unfinished when work was suspended at the outbreak of war.

- **Temple of Athena Nike** (Greek: Ναός Αθηνάς Νίκης; modern: Temple of Athena Nike)
  Period: ca. 427–424 BCE · Region: acropolis · State: new
  Evidence: archaeological
  Position / footprint: small amphiprostyle Ionic temple ~5 × 8 m on the bastion west of the Propylaea
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Athena_Nike
  Description: Tetrastyle amphiprostyle Ionic temple designed by Kallikrates, finished ca. 420 BCE during the Peace of Nicias.

- **Erechtheion** (Greek: Ἐρέχθειον; modern: Erechtheion)
  Period: 421–406 BCE; commissioned in this window; under construction here · Region: acropolis · State: working
  Evidence: archaeological
  Position / footprint: ~22 × 11 m; Pentelic marble with Eleusinian limestone frieze
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erechtheion
  Description: Ionic temple begun 421 BCE, dedicated 406 BCE. In the period 450–430 BCE it is under construction (we mark it `working`); the olive-wood Athena Polias cult statue stood here and the Panathenaic peplos was delivered here during the Great Panathenaia.

- **Erechtheion North Porch** (Πρόστυλον)
  Period: 421–406 BCE; under construction · Region: acropolis · State: working
  Evidence: documented
  Position / footprint: ~8 × 5 m
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erechtheion
  Description: Prostyle east-facing porch in Pentelic marble; the olive-tree cult of Athena Polias stood here.

- **Erechtheion Karyatid Porch** (Πόρτις μετά Κορών)
  Period: 421–406 BCE; under construction · Region: acropolis · State: working
  Evidence: documented
  Position / footprint: ~6 × 4 m south porch
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erechtheion
  Description: South porch with six draped maidens (Karyatids) carrying the Ionic entablature; under construction in this period.

- **Chalkotheke** (Greek: Χαλκοθήκη; modern: Chalkotheke)
  Period: 5th c. BCE · Region: acropolis · State: standing
  Evidence: documented
  Position / footprint: ~30 × 11 m, Doric stoa-like building south-east of the Erechtheion
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acropolis_of_Athens
  Description: Long rectangular Doric building: state treasury for votive offerings and bronze.

- **Sanctuary of Artemis Brauronia** (Greek: Βραυρώνειον; modern: Sanctuary of Artemis Brauronia)
  Period: 5th c. BCE · Region: acropolis · State: standing
  Evidence: archaeological
  Position / footprint: ~16 × 12 m on the Acropolis
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acropolis_of_Athens
  Description: Doric stoa-like sanctuary on the Acropolis; a near-copy of the rural sanctuary at Brauron.

- **Sanctuary of Zeus Polieus** (Greek: Βωμὸς Διὸς Πολιέως; modern: Bomos of Zeus Polieus)
  Period: archaic, in use throughout the 5th c. · Region: acropolis · State: standing
  Evidence: documented
  Position / footprint: ~12 × 12 m open-air precinct north of the Erechtheion
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acropolis_of_Athens
  Description: Open-air precinct with the altar of Zeus Polieus and the Bouphonia sacrificial pit.

- **Athena Promachos** (Greek: Ἀθην� Πρόμαχος; modern: Athena Promachos)
  Period: ca. 460 BCE onwards · Region: acropolis · State: standing
  Evidence: documented
  Position / footprint: ~9 m tall bronze statue between the Propylaea and the Erechtheion
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenon
  Description: Tall bronze Athena by Phidias, dedicated after the Persian Wars; her helmet and spear were visible from ships rounding Sunium.

## SOUTH SLOPE OF THE ACROPOLIS

- **Theatre of Dionysus** (Greek: Θέατρον Διονύσου; modern: Theatre of Dionysus)
  Period: 5th c. BCE, ongoing enlargement · Region: south-slope · State: working
  Evidence: archaeological
  Position / footprint: cavea ~60 × 80 m; orchestra ~18 m diameter
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_Dionysus
  Description: In 450–430 BCE the cavea is stone-seated and the proskenion is wooden (the Lykourgan stone phase belongs to the mid-4th c.). Site of the City Dionysia.

- **Sanctuary of Dionysus Eleuthereus** (Greek: Ἱερὸν Διονύσου Ἐλευθερέως; modern: Sanctuary of Dionysus Eleuthereus)
  Period: 5th c. BCE · Region: south-slope · State: standing
  Evidence: archaeological
  Position / footprint: ~40 × 18 m south of the rock
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_Dionysus
  Description: The older sanctuary beneath the Acropolis rock; temple of Dionysus Eleuthereus with choragic monuments.

- **Odeion of Pericles** (Greek: Ὠδεῖον Περικλέους; modern: Odeion of Pericles)
  Period: ca. 443 BCE · Region: south-slope · State: new
  Evidence: documented
  Position / footprint: ~30 × 28 m square hall
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_of_Pericles
  Description: Square hall roofed with ship-timber masts captured from the Persian fleet, imitating Xerxes’ tent; used for musical contests and the City Dionysia rehearsals.

- **Asclepieion** (Greek: Ἀσκληπιεῖον; modern: Asclepieion)
  Period: 5th c. BCE · Region: south-slope · State: standing
  Evidence: archaeological
  Position / footprint: ~40 × 30 m; spring, kline hall and temple
  SOURCES: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acropolis_of_Athens
  Description: Sanctuary of Asclepius on the south slope below the Acropolis; pilgrimage and incubation healing site.

## Out of scope

- Eleusis proper (the town), Marathon battlefield, Laurion silver mines — too distant to depict in this reconstruction
- Hellenistic Athens, Roman Athens, Byzantine Athens, Ottoman Athens
- Peloponnesian War events after 430 BCE
- The Plague of Athens (it begins at the boundary and is therefore not depicted)

## Caveats

- **Erechtheion is under construction in this period** (421–406 BCE). It is shown as `working` with the north porch and Karyatid porch also under construction.
- **The Olympieion is the small archaic naos**, not the later Roman giant. The 174 BCE temple by Cossutius and the Roman completion under Hadrian are out of period.
- **Piraeus is shown as a separate connected harbour city** via the Long Walls corridor, not as a suburb of Athens.
- **The Themistoclean walls are standing**. The Konon re-build of 394 BCE is out of period and not depicted; we show only the Themistoclean circuit.
- **The Long Walls shown are the Themistoclean north wall (478 BCE) and the middle / southern wall (ca. 443 BCE)**. The Kimonian early wall of 461 BCE (demolished 461 BCE) is not separately depicted.
- **The Plague of Athens begins in 430 BCE**; we stop at the boundary and do not depict the Plague.
- **Population numbers vary widely**: 30–40k adult male citizens is the consensus range; total Attic population estimates of 150k–250k+ depend on assumptions.
- **Pericles' citizenship law of 451 BCE** restricting citizenship to children of two Athenian parents is documented but its enforcement date is debated.
- **Building dimensions are taken from Wikipedia / academic.cite references**; local Travlos or ASCSA numbers may differ slightly.
- **ASCSA Agora Excavations canonical URL** is `https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/excavations/athenian-agora/about-the-excavations/agorahistory the historical `agora.ascsa.gr` redirect was not always reachable from all networks.
